Definition matters more than sampling error. The point estimates span about 15 points across thresholds, while each CI is under 3 points wide. The CIs capture sampling noise only, not the choice of cutoff.
Churn is inferred, not observed. Inter-purchase gaps have a median of 32 days, p75 of 70, and p90 of 148. A 90-day cutoff therefore classifies some customers with normally long gaps as churned.
Censoring grows with the threshold, from 354 customers at 60 days to 900 at 180. Dropping censored customers removes recent, likely-active buyers from the denominator, which may push the rates up. I haven’t verified the direction.
Seasonality is not modeled. The window covers roughly two years of a gift-oriented retailer, which could affect gap lengths.

Non-causal caveats:

The segment definition is partly circular. Wholesale_Proxy is defined over the full observation window as average invoice revenue ≥ £300 or lifetime quantity ≥ 1,000 units. Lifetime quantity accumulates across repeat orders, so repeat buyers are more likely to be labeled Wholesale_Proxy by construction.
The labels are proxies, not confirmed account types.
The metric is “active in month N,” not survival. Retail customers can return after gaps, so the curves are not monotonic.
The comparison is observational. It says nothing about what would happen if a customer were moved between segments.
(d) Testable hypothesis: At-Risk cohort
Cohort: 324 customers (£1,390,289.84 lifetime revenue) with ≥3 purchase days, ≤180 days since last purchase, and overdue beyond max(1.5 × median gap, median gap + 14). The top 10 by revenue are all UK Wholesale_Proxy and hold £335,040.39, about 24% of the cohort total.

Hypothesis: A personalised reorder prompt sent when a customer is flagged at-risk will raise the share who reorder within 30 days, compared with no outreach.

Primary metric: the share of customers placing at least one valid order, under the same exclusion rules, within 30 days of assignment.
Control: a randomized holdout with no outreach, 1:1 assignment, stratified by lifetime-revenue tercile.
Secondary metrics: revenue per customer at 30 and 60 days.
Guardrails: discount cost and opt-out or complaint rate.
Power: at about 162 per arm, the minimum detectable effect is roughly 15 percentage points absolute at 80% power, assuming a baseline near 40%. That is my approximation, and the true baseline is unknown. This is underpowered for modest lifts, so consider a longer enrollment window.
Revenue concentration: the top accounts dominate revenue, so report a revenue-weighted result as well. You may also want to handle the very top accounts separately.
Applicability: the cohort comes from the frozen 2011 snapshot, so the test would apply the same flagging rule to a live customer base.
